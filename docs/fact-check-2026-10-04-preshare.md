## Where Stuart is most likely to push back

1. **"Hop" = card = one step (R1).** He wrote the 0.02 s / 50-steps-per-card structure and the drag-table design. This is the one place the exhibit misdescribes his own program.
2. **"Recreation of the 1946 demo" (R4).** His paper hedges it as a hypothesis; the video presents it as the demo.
3. **"The real ENIAC pulse by pulse" (N1) and "You programmed ENIAC" (N3).** Fine in spirit; he may ask for his own phrasing.
4. **Drag table.** Your trajectory-history.md 5.3 already notes the function table's jump sits near launch speed, not Mach 1, and that the run lands ~12,900 yd at 22.5 degrees (long for a 105 mm). Neither claim appears on a visitor surface (good). If Stuart asks "is the drag realistic?", that note is the honest answer: ask him; don't volunteer a range.
5. **The unveiling credit.** The kiosk correctly names no one for the 15 Feb trajectory (Jennings/Snyder per Bartik/Fritz/Light vs Wescoff/Lichterman per Penn). Good; keep it unnamed.
6. **Stuart thank-you copy.** honor.ts `fc` says the wording is "to go to Brian Stuart with the R1 request (Q18)". "Without his work, this exhibit would be an empty screen" and N05 go out to him in the video first; make sure he sees it as a thank-you, not a quote.
