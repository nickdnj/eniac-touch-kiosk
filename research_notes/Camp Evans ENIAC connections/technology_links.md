# Signal Corps / Camp Evans radar work and ENIAC: technology, staff, funding, administration

Bottom line: the only documented Signal Corps thread is a Moore School radar-antenna *computing contract* (Brainerd's "project PZ") that Mauchly says motivated him to push for an electronic computer. No source I could reach ties ENIAC's circuits, funding or personnel to Camp Evans / Evans Signal Laboratory by name. Primary access limits: I could not read Haigh/Priestley/Rope, Stern, Goldstine, McCartney, Burks, or the full Brainerd 1976 article (see Gaps).

Source quality key: A = primary/first-hand transcript; B = reputable secondary or institutional; C = museum/advocacy, forum or tertiary, needs corroboration.

## 1. Electronics (pulse circuits, counters, delay lines, thyratron/tube circuits): where did the ideas come from?

### Takeaway
Documented origin of Eckert's delay-line work is radar-timing / moving-target-indication work associated with the MIT Radiation Laboratory, done at the Moore School; Eckert's ENIAC counter circuits are described by Mauchly as a survey of published circuits plus reliability testing. I found nothing attributing any of this to Camp Evans.

### Cited Findings
- Mauchly (primary, 1973): at ENIAC start, Eckert "started gathering all of the information he could" on published/available electronic counter circuits and tested them for reliability, taking a couple of months. No Signal Corps or radar source named. [Mauchly oral history, Smithsonian AC0196, 10 Jan 1973 (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Brainerd 1976, as quoted secondhand: lists earlier Moore School work by Chedaker, Eckert and Sharpless "on advanced electronic circuits, electronic counters, and delay lines on project PL." [Quoted in arXiv 1908.09549, Oprema paper (B, secondhand)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.09549); original: Brainerd, "Genesis of the ENIAC," Technology and Culture 17(3), 1976, [listing](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/891755/summary)
- Mercury delay line: first practical radar de-cluttering (moving-target) system attributed to Eckert at the Moore School; later applied to computer memory (EDVAC/UNIVAC). [Wikipedia, Delay-line memory (B-/C; cites Eckert 1953 Proc. IRE, Isaacson)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delay-line_memory)
- Search-result summaries of NAE Memorial Tributes and other pages say Eckert's mercury tank for radar timing arose "from a request by MIT's Radiation Laboratory," under a Moore School contract with the Rad Lab (switching amplifier). I did not fetch the underlying page, so treat as unverified. [NAE Memorial Tributes vol. 10](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10403/chapter/14)
- Eckert "participated in research on radar timing" at the Moore School; no Signal Corps or Rad Lab named there. [Wikipedia, J. Presper Eckert (C)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Presper_Eckert)
- Eckert "worked in radar" and taught Army electronics classes at the Moore School; no lab named. [Linda Hall Library (B)](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/j-presper-eckert/)
- Mauchly met Eckert in the 1941 Moore School summer defense course (Eckert ran the lab sessions). [Mauchly oral history (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)

### Inferences
- Documented lineage for delay lines = Moore School radar-timing work for the MIT Rad Lab (plausible link to OSRD/NDRC contracting). The "PL" label is unverified as being the Rad Lab contract.
- Plausible only: general Signal Corps radar know-how circulating in the wartime community. No document ties ENIAC circuit design to Camp Evans.
- Do not credit delay lines to Camp Evans: the delay line was not used in ENIAC itself (it was EDVAC-era), and its radar origin is Rad Lab-linked.

### Gaps
- Original Brainerd 1976 text, Eckert's 1944-45 progress reports, Stern, Burks, Goldstine, McCartney and Haigh et al. not accessed (paywalled or not retrievable).
- Not verified that project PL was the Rad Lab subcontract.

## 2. Did Camp Evans research (antenna, Diana, SCR-270/584, Signal Corps Radar Lab) supply techniques, components, or personnel?

### Takeaway
No. The only documented transfer is flow in the opposite direction: the Signal Corps (location given only as "New Jersey") sent the Moore School a computation job. The "Camp Evans" label is added by a museum, not by Mauchly.

### Cited Findings
- Mauchly: Brainerd negotiated "with the Signal Corps in New Jersey" a contract studying radiation patterns of a parabolic reflector with a dipole at its focus, for radar target definition; solved by numerical integration. Part of the contract was experimental (others built and tested a reflector); Burks was assigned. [Mauchly oral history (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Mauchly: only one desk calculator was available, so he sought Ursinus's machine and more calculators via a priority obtained through the Signal Corps; the Moore School ended with three. [same (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Mauchly: he was "impelled" back to the computer idea by slow hand calculation on the Signal Corps job; the Signal Corps was satisfied and gave a follow-on. [same (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Brainerd 1976 (quoted secondhand): "Burks and John Mauchly were involved in related radar work on project PZ (a Signal Corps project obtained by Brainerd)." [arXiv 1908.09549 (B)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.09549)
- InfoAge (Camp Evans museum, by Evan Koblentz) claims parabolic antenna research at Camp Evans "was part of the reason ENIAC happened," citing Mauchly's transcripts; the page's Mauchly quotes never name Camp Evans. [InfoAge (C)](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/)
- Koblentz 2011 list post: Camp Evans "had a role in ENIAC" and was a customer of the Moore School differential analyzer in 1947; he cites no source and the thread has no replies. [SIGCIS list (C, unverified)](https://lists.sigcis.org/archives/list/members@lists.sigcis.org/thread/4Z4TGLWORM2BI55FYIDKQJ5F4BUHEGBV/)
- Camp Evans was a Signal Corps radar R&D site, named 1942. [Wikipedia, Camp Evans Historic District (C)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Evans_Historic_District)

### Inferences
- Plausible: the Signal Corps client in NJ could have been Camp Evans (Signal Corps Laboratories sat in the Fort Monmouth complex), but Mauchly names only "New Jersey" and Fort Monmouth is not the same as Camp Evans; unproven.
- The contract gave a motive (computation burden), not techniques, components or hardware to ENIAC. No personnel moved from Evans to the Moore School in any source found.
- Project Diana, SCR-270/584 and the Signal Corps Radar Lab: no source tied these to the Moore School.

### Gaps
- Contract number, sponsor office, and site for project PZ unknown.
- Whether the 1947 analyzer use by Camp Evans is real: no primary source.
- Nothing found on personnel exchange.

## 3. Funding and administration

### Takeaway
ENIAC was an Army Ordnance contract supervised by the Ballistic Research Laboratory. The Army Ordnance history does not mention the Signal Corps, radar, Camp Evans or OSRD. The only Signal Corps administrative touch is the calculator priority.

### Cited Findings
- Contract W-670-ORD-4926 (R.A.D. 1078), dated 5 June 1943, initial $61,700 Ordnance funds; nine supplements brought total to $486,804.22 and put technical supervision under BRL, Aberdeen. [Army Ordnance history, ch. 2 (B)](https://ed-thelen.org/comp-hist/U-S-Ord-61-ch02.html)
- Same source: Gillon, Goldstine, Simon and Feltman credited with securing Ordnance support; the Moore School analyzer was already used "exclusively for the army" from 1942. [same (B)](https://ed-thelen.org/comp-hist/U-S-Ord-61-ch02.html); fuller context via [computinghistory.org.uk](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/det/6148/Work-begins-on-ENIAC/)
- Brainerd was principal investigator and administrative lead, managing about $486,000. [IEEE CS pioneers (B)](https://history.computer.org/pioneers/brainerd.html)
- Mauchly says Brainerd did most of the outside contract work and contact with "the Signal Corps and the Army Ordnance people"; Brainerd/Eckert visited Gillon at the Pentagon. [Mauchly oral history (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Moore School operated the Bush differential analyzer for the Army from June 1942; Sept 1942 substation of Aberdeen. [Army Ordnance history (B)](https://ed-thelen.org/comp-hist/U-S-Ord-61-ch02.html)

### Inferences
- Documented funding/administration is Ordnance only. Signal Corps funded a separate, earlier contract that shared faculty (Brainerd, Mauchly, Burks) with ENIAC.
- No OSRD or Office of the Chief Signal Officer support for ENIAC found. Absence in the sources reached is not proof of absence.

### Gaps
- Did not access Stern's contract-by-contract treatment, Goldstine's account, or the 1946 Report/progress reports.
- No document located on OSRD/NDRC review of ENIAC.

## 4. What do the standard accounts say about the Signal Corps role?

### Takeaway
Only Mauchly (primary) and Brainerd (via quote) were reachable. Both place the Signal Corps as a client for a computation-heavy contract that preceded ENIAC. I could not check Stern, Goldstine, Burks, McCartney, Haigh et al. or Eckert directly.

### Cited Findings
- Mauchly (see sections 2-3) is the first-hand account; he explicitly treats the Signal Corps job as motivation, not a source of technology. [Mauchly oral history (A)](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Brainerd classifies PZ as "related radar work," separate from the circuit-development line (PL). [arXiv 1908.09549 (B)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.09549)
- Search tool reports (unverified) that Haigh/Priestley/Rope text was not surfaced; no excerpt available.

### Inferences
- Documented: Signal Corps contract preceded and motivated the computer proposal. Plausible: it also supplied the Moore School's early reputation/capacity for classified government work.

### Gaps
- Stern (1981), Goldstine (1972), McCartney (1999), Haigh/Priestley/Rope (2016), Burks and Eckert's own accounts, Sharpless/Brainerd/Goldstine Smithsonian transcripts: not read. Mauchly's 6 Feb 1973 transcript URL (amhistory.si.edu) returned 404; the mads.si.edu PDF was read.
- Not searched: archive.org full texts, ETHW, MIT Rad Lab histories.
