# Which Signal Corps laboratory sponsored the Moore School antenna/radar contract Mauchly worked on (1941-43)?

## Which Signal Corps organization held the contract, and is it Camp Evans / Evans Signal Laboratory?

### Takeaway
No document I could access names the sponsoring laboratory. The only contemporaneous-participant sources say "the Signal Corps in New Jersey" (Mauchly) and "project PZ, a Signal Corps project" (Brainerd, via a secondary quotation). Camp Evans is a plausible but unproven inference; the claim is currently supported only by InfoAge, which rests solely on Mauchly's transcript, which does not name Evans.

### Cited Findings
- Mauchly says Brainerd negotiated "with the Signal Corps in New Jersey" a contract studying a parabolic reflector with a dipole radiator at its focus; aim was sharper radar target definition; classified. Primary (oral history, recalled ~30 years later), quality: high for what he recalls, moderate for detail. — [Smithsonian NMAH AC0196, Mauchly/Tropp 10 Jan 1973](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf) (I extracted the PDF text locally and searched it)
- The same transcript contains no occurrence of "Evans", "Monmouth", "Belmar", "Red Bank" or "Camp" (text search of the full 3,668-line extraction returned none). The only location given is "New Jersey". — same source
- Mauchly: "I don't know that I ever actually saw the contract", and he cannot recall the time period; Brainerd had set a roughly six-month target, which was overrun. — same source
- Brainerd, in 1976, listed "project PZ (a Signal Corps project obtained by Brainerd)" on which Burks and Mauchly did "related radar work", and "project PL" for Chedaker/Eckert/Sharpless electronic circuits, counters and delay lines. Quoted second-hand in a scholarly paper citing J. G. Brainerd, "Genesis of the ENIAC", Technology and Culture 17(3), Jul 1976, pp. 482-488 (pp. 486-487). Quality: secondary quotation of a primary participant account; I did not see the original. — [arXiv 1908.09549 (Oprema paper)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.09549)
- The Oprema paper's authors caution that Brainerd's 1976 account is not fully reliable on one other point (Mauchly's Ursinus artifacts). — same source
- InfoAge states "Parabolic radar antenna research here at Camp Evans" contributed to ENIAC's development. — [InfoAge, Koblentz](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/); same text also at [infoage.org/?p=923](https://infoage.org/?p=923). Quality: advocacy/museum site, no independent documentation.
- Camp Evans naming: sources conflict on when it became Evans Signal Laboratory (1941/42 vs 1945), per search summaries; not verified by me. — [Signal Corps Laboratories (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps_Laboratories); [DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/461381/project-diana-inspires-future-technologies-10-jan-1946)

### Inferences
- "Signal Corps in New Jersey" in 1941-42 could mean Evans Signal Laboratory (Belmar), other Signal Corps Laboratories elements at Fort Monmouth, or a Signal Corps procurement/engineering office; the words alone do not discriminate. Radar R&D was centered at Camp Evans/Fort Monmouth, so Evans is plausible but not demonstrated.
- The contract identifier is probably "PZ" (a Moore School internal project code, parallel to PL and PX for the ENIAC/Ordnance work), not a Signal Corps contract number. This is my inference from Brainerd's list; unconfirmed.

## Moore School personnel, dates, and scope

### Takeaway
Mauchly (theory, numerical integration), Brainerd (obtained contract), Burks (assigned to project, in touch with both parts), Sharpless (experimental side) worked on it; scope was radiation patterns of paraboloid plus dipole, analysed by step-by-step integration with desk calculators; work overlapped with early ENIAC (1943).

### Cited Findings
- Contract was partly experimental: others built a physical reflector and tested it, in parallel with the computation; Burks was assigned and also kept in touch with Mauchly's work. — [AC0196 Mauchly 10 Jan 1973](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Sharpless "was one of the people who had worked on this Signal Corps project", on the experimental side. — same source
- Mauchly says he still had not finished the Signal Corps radiation-pattern computing when the ENIAC project started, so he finished them during early ENIAC work. — same source
- Only one desk calculator was available at first; the work was done by hand/desk machines and motivated his push for electronic computing. — same source
- Koblentz's article, per fetch summary, cites only Smithsonian transcripts mauc730110 and mauc730206 plus the VCF exhibit; no other document names Camp Evans. — [InfoAge](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/)

### Inferences
- Start 1941 (after summer course) through roughly 1943-44 is implied by Mauchly's account; exact dates are not given in anything I read.
