# People-level links between Camp Evans / Fort Monmouth Signal Corps labs and the ENIAC project (1940-1948)

Source quality key: A = primary (first-person transcript); B = institutional secondary; C = informal/forum; D = search-tool summary only, not verified by me.

## Key Question 1: Did a Signal Corps officer/scientist from Camp Evans/Fort Monmouth visit, consult on, demand time on, or review ENIAC?

### Takeaway
No document found naming any Signal Corps NJ officer or scientist (Zahl, Colton, DeWitt, Blair, Ziegler, Clark, etc.) visiting, reviewing, or requesting ENIAC time. The only documented person-level link is the Signal Corps *contract* that Mauchly worked on at the Moore School, whose Signal Corps counterparts are never named in the transcript.

### Cited Findings
- Mauchly (Tropp interview, 10 Jan 1973) says Moore School's Dr. Brainerd "was able to make negotiations with the Signal Corps in New Jersey" for a contract studying parabolic reflector/dipole radiation patterns (classified, radar). Source quality A (transcript line ~729-760). — [Mauchly interview, Smithsonian AC0196](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Same transcript: Brainerd "did most of the outside contract work and contact with the Signal Corps and the Army Ordnance people." (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Same transcript: the Signal Corps "was only interested in getting its results"; no Signal Corps person is named; the lab is given only as "the Signal Corps in New Jersey" (not Camp Evans or Fort Monmouth by name). (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Mauchly says Brainerd and others thought they could not get a priority to order a desk calculator, as it would be hard to persuade "the Signal Corps or somebody" to supply the papers; they eventually got one that way. (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- InfoAge (Camp Evans museum) article, by Evan Koblentz, frames ENIAC as "impelled" by the Camp Evans project; this is the article's own framing resting on Mauchly quotes. (B, advocacy-leaning, undated) — [InfoAge](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/)
- Harold Zahl: InfoAge biography has him Director of Research at Fort Monmouth from 1948 to 1966, i.e. after the ENIAC period; no ENIAC mention found in the 2007 InfoAge biography PDF I text-extracted. (B/D) — [InfoAge Zahl PDF](https://www.infoage.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/014_2007_harold_zahl.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because the Moore School contract came via "the Signal Corps in New Jersey" in 1942, the Evans Signal Laboratory (Belmar/Wall, renamed 1941, per InfoAge) or Fort Monmouth headquarters are plausible but unproven counterparts. Do not name a specific officer.
- The kiosk should say "a Signal Corps radar contract" rather than "Camp Evans" unless a document names the lab.

### Gaps
- Identity of the Signal Corps technical monitor(s) for the Moore School antenna contract: not in transcript; check Moore School contract files (Penn archives) or Signal Corps histories.
- No document found for Col. Clark, Colton, DeWitt, Blair, Ziegler or Zahl visiting ENIAC; I did not access Terrett/Thompson volumes, Stern, Goldstine, Burks or the 1946 Report text. Absence of evidence in a limited search, not proof.
- Haigh/Priestley/Rope (ENIAC in Action) index not checked.

## Key Question 2: Did the Signal Corps send problems to ENIAC at Aberdeen (1946-47) or request Moore School computations?

### Takeaway
Antenna radiation-pattern computations were requested of the Moore School (by hand/desk calculator, c. 1942-43), before ENIAC existed. No documented Signal Corps problem on ENIAC itself was found.

### Cited Findings
- The Signal Corps antenna job was computed by hand with desk machines (Monroe from Ursinus, one extra Moore calculator via priority); Marlene Wescott was hired as one of Mauchly's "computers" on it, doing step-by-step integrations. (A) — [Mauchly interview](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Mauchly says he had not finished the Signal Corps project when the ENIAC project began and had to finish radiation patterns concurrently. (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- A SIGCIS list post by Koblentz (17 Apr 2011) claims Camp Evans in 1947 was a customer of the Moore School's differential analyzer, and in 1956 moved on to MOBIDIC; no source cited. (C) — [SIGCIS thread](https://lists.sigcis.org/archives/list/members@lists.sigcis.org/thread/4Z4TGLWORM2BI55FYIDKQJ5F4BUHEGBV/)
- Separate: the Moore School built MSAC for the Army Signal Corps in 1951 (outside the 1940-48 window). (B) — Hagley finding aid, https://findingaids.hagley.org/repositories/3/resources/1853 (via search summary, D)

### Inferences
- Differential analyzer use in 1947 is the only claimed post-war computation request and is unverified; it is not ENIAC.

### Gaps
- No ENIAC job list entry attributed to the Signal Corps found. ARL ENIAC/comphist pages (ftp.arl.mil/~mike/comphist) were returned in search but I did not read them in full. Check ENIAC in Action's problem catalogue.

## Key Question 3: Did any ENIAC engineer/mathematician work at, or previously work at, Camp Evans / Fort Monmouth?

### Takeaway
Several ENIAC people worked on the Signal Corps antenna contract at the Moore School (not at Camp Evans). No ENIAC team member documented as moving to or from Camp Evans/SCEL.

### Cited Findings
- Kite Sharpless "was one of the people who had worked on this Signal Corps project, on the experimental end"; and was put onto ENIAC when contract work allowed. (A) — [Mauchly interview](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Arthur Burks "had been on the Signal Corps project" before ENIAC. (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Mauchly says Professor Witmer was brought in to help on the Signal Corps radiation pattern job. (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Mauchly himself and Marlene Wescott (computer) worked on it; Wescott is not stated to have been an ENIAC programmer. (A) — [same](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Brainerd later supervised an Automatic Data Retrieval System for a Signal Corps project (post-1948). (D, via search summary) — [ETHW Brainerd](https://ethw.org/John_G._Brainerd)
- Oliver Reynolds, c. 88 in 2011, said he built a tube computer at Camp Evans after WWII; no detail, no ENIAC tie, speculative. (C) — [SIGCIS](https://lists.sigcis.org/archives/list/members@lists.sigcis.org/thread/4Z4TGLWORM2BI55FYIDKQJ5F4BUHEGBV/)

### Inferences
- Sharpless and Burks give the strongest documented person-level link: the Signal Corps contract was a feeder of staff into ENIAC. Note that the contract was at the Moore School, not at Camp Evans.

### Gaps
- Did not check Eckert, Chuan Chu, Huskey, Cummings, Davis, Chedaker, Shaw or the six programmers for later Fort Monmouth careers. Search surfaced nothing; not exhaustively searched.
