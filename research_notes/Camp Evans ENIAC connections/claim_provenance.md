# Provenance of the InfoAge/VCF claim that Camp Evans is linked to ENIAC

## Where does the claim come from, and what is the Koblentz article's basis?

### Takeaway
The claim traces to one primary source: Mauchly's 10 Jan 1973 Smithsonian oral history. In it Mauchly says a Signal Corps contract "in New Jersey" (radar parabolic-reflector patterns) pushed him toward electronic computing. The transcript never names Camp Evans (I searched the full text for "Evans" and "Monmouth": zero hits). The identification of that Signal Corps client as Camp Evans is Koblentz/InfoAge/VCF's inference, and the article cites no document beyond the oral history.

### Cited Findings
- Koblentz article: author Evan Koblentz (senior writer/NJIT adjunct); venue InfoAge website history section; page says "Original Publication Date Unknown"; no contract numbers or documents named besides two Smithsonian transcript PDFs (mauc730110, mauc730206) and the VCF ENIAC exhibit. Two URL variants serve the same article. — [infoage.org article](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/), [alt URL](https://infoage.org/history/computer-research/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/) (source quality: primary for what InfoAge says; secondary for history)
- The article itself says the Signal Corps project was "from New Jersey"; per my fetch summary it does not explicitly identify the site as Camp Evans in the body, even though the title and the "part of the reason ENIAC happened" sentence assert it. — [infoage.org article](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/) (summarized by fetch tool; verify against live page)
- Mauchly transcript (Tropp interview, 10 Jan 1973): Dr. Brainerd (Brainard in transcript) negotiated with "the Signal Corps in New Jersey" a contract studying a parabolic reflector with dipole radiator, done by hand numerical integration; Mauchly says "I don't know that I ever actually saw the contract". Project was classified. — [Smithsonian NMAH AC0196 transcript](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110) (primary, high quality; I read the PDF text locally)
- Same transcript: Mauchly says he was "impelled" back to the electronic-computer idea by slow hand calculation on "the Signal Corps project"; borrowed desk calculators from Wharton and tried Ursinus; was still finishing the Signal Corps patterns while ENIAC began. — [same transcript](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Same transcript names the Moore School's Signal Corps contact only as "the Signal Corps" and "the Signal Corps in New Jersey"; no Camp Evans, Evans Signal Laboratory, Fort Monmouth, contract number. — [same transcript](https://mads.si.edu/mads/id/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110)
- Only the Jan 1973 transcript was checked; the second linked transcript (mauc730206) and the third part were NOT read. See Gaps.
- VCF page (author and date not stated; footer 2015-2026) repeats it as "impelled ... Camp Evans", cites Smithsonian SOVA AC0196, the-eniac.com, 1977 Trenton Computer Festival lecture and VCF East 5.0 lecture by Bill Mauchly, campevans.org, infoage.org. — [vcfed.org/?p=291](https://vcfed.org/?p=291) (source quality: museum-affiliated, secondary)

### Inferences
- The "Camp Evans" label is an interpretive step: "Signal Corps in New Jersey" fits Camp Evans/Evans Signal Laboratory (radar lab, Fort Monmouth system) but could also be Signal Corps Laboratories HQ at Fort Monmouth or another Signal Corps office. Mauchly's words do not discriminate.
- The title's "impelled" is Mauchly's quote about his own motivation, applied by InfoAge to the Camp Evans project, which makes the headline stronger than the source.

### Gaps
- No Wayback captures could be fetched (web.archive.org blocked in this environment); true publication dates unknown; InfoAge pages self-report "Original Publication Date Unknown".
- Transcript mauc730206 and part 3 not read; a later part might name the lab.
- Whether the Bill Mauchly lectures (1977 Trenton, VCF East 5.0) say "Camp Evans" explicitly: not accessed.

## What other InfoAge/VCF/Koblentz statements exist, mapped to sources?

### Takeaway
Statements cluster in four places and all trace back to the same Mauchly transcript plus Koblentz's own assertions; the Computer Research page makes stronger, unsourced claims (inspired ENIAC developers; ASCII; screens; mobile computers).

### Cited Findings
- InfoAge "Computer Research" page (no author/date; footer 2023): campus in computer research "from about 1941-1957"; projects "helped inspire the ENIAC developers"; loaned technology to early computers with output screens; designed earliest non-prototype mobile computers; "directly led to ASCII." No citations on the page; links the Koblentz article. — [infoage.org Computer Research](https://www.infoage.org/history-ia/computer-research/) (primary for InfoAge's claims; no sourcing)
- VCF page: Fieldata made at Camp Evans and "basis for ASCII"; MOBIDIC built 1956 in two 30-foot trailers; 1953 CRT demonstrations to IBM and MIT leading to Whirlwind II/SAGE; Micro-Module test site 1957; cites Wikipedia SAGE, campevans.org, VCF East 5.0 lecture. — [vcfed.org/?p=291](https://vcfed.org/?p=291)
- Koblentz, SIGCIS list, 17 Apr 2011: Camp Evans "did have a role in ENIAC" (as explained earlier on the list), "in 1947 was a customer of the Moore School's differential analyzer", moved to MOBIDIC in 1956; no replies in thread. This is the earliest dated Koblentz statement found. — [SIGCIS thread](https://lists.sigcis.org/archives/list/members@lists.sigcis.org/thread/4Z4TGLWORM2BI55FYIDKQJ5F4BUHEGBV/) (author's own assertion, unsourced)
- Koblentz 2008 vcf-midatlantic post mentions MOBIDIC and Bill Mauchly speaking but not Camp Evans/ENIAC link. — [vcf-midatlantic post](https://lists.sigcis.org/archives/list/members@lists.sigcis.org/thread/JQQD2RWP2335YNDCU4LVPBPHCAHKRHS4/)
- InfoAge WWII Radar page and the NHL nomination (NPS historian Robie Lange, c.2011-12) do not mention ENIAC/Moore School in the text I could read (first 100k of 131k chars of nomination). The nomination mentions the site's later use "as a computer laboratory". — [WWII Radar](https://www.infoage.org/history-ia/world-war-ii-radar/), [NHL nomination](https://www.infoage.org/history-ia/historical-resources/camp-evans-national-historic-landmark-nomination/)
- Wikipedia's Camp Evans Historic District article does not mention ENIAC. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Evans_Historic_District)

### Inferences
- Likely chain: Mauchly 1973 -> Koblentz (by 2011) -> InfoAge/VCF pages. "1947 customer of differential analyzer" is a separate, unsourced claim about a later date (after ENIAC).

### Gaps
- The "earlier list explanation" Koblentz references was not located.
- No page found giving a Moore School contract number tied to Camp Evans.
- 2nd half of NHL nomination unread.

## Is there independent corroboration or contrary scholarship?

### Takeaway
I found no independent archival or scholarly source naming Camp Evans/Evans Signal Laboratory in connection with the Moore School or ENIAC. Standard accounts identify Army Ordnance (BRL) as ENIAC's sponsor. The Signal Corps radar-pattern job is genuine per Mauchly but is a side project, not the ENIAC contract.

### Cited Findings
- ENIAC contract was an Army Ordnance contract (April 1943; six-month initial $61,700 per one account); preceded by 1942 Ordnance contract for the differential analyzer. — [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/tdih/april/9/), [Ed Thelen Ordnance history](https://ed-thelen.org/comp-hist/U-S-Ord-61-ch02.html), [Penn Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/collections/finding-aid/upd8_4/) (search-result summaries; not read in full)
- Searches for Camp Evans/Evans Signal Laboratory plus Moore School/ENIAC returned no linkage in Penn Archives, CHM, Wikipedia, Army ENIAC histories. — web searches above; negative result, not proof of absence
- Camp Evans renamed Evans Signal Laboratory April 1945. — [Wikipedia Signal Corps Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps_Laboratories) (secondary)
- Fieldata: Signal Corps late-1950s program, MOBIDIC contract 1956 via Fort Monmouth, influenced ASCII; ASCII committee included a Fieldata member; ASCII first released 1963. Influence, not "directly led to". — [Wikipedia Fieldata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fieldata), [MOBIDIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOBIDIC) (secondary; Sylvania built MOBIDIC)
- No scholarship specifically discussing or disputing the InfoAge claim was found.

### Inferences
- Claim is best classed as "supported by one participant recollection (Mauchly), site identification inferred, not corroborated by archival document found." Not refuted either.
- "Directly led to ASCII" overstates; "influenced" is supported. MOBIDIC by Sylvania, so "Camp Evans designed" is arguable (contract management vs. design).

### Gaps
- National Archives RG 111 / Penn University Archives Moore School contract files not searched (not online/accessible here).
- Hagley/Penn Brainerd papers, Stern's "From ENIAC to UNIVAC", Goldstine, Burks not checked for a Signal Corps/Evans mention.
- "1941-1957" and "output screens" have no source on the pages; origin unidentified (1941 matches Army acquisition of Camp Evans per NHL nomination; 1957 matches Micro-Module per VCF page: inference only).
