# Programmers' and colleagues' accounts of Marlyn Wescoff's recruitment and pre-ENIAC work

Method note: I could not access Wescoff's own interview transcripts (Kleiman/ENIAC Programmers Project, 1997), Bartik's "Pioneer Programmer", Kleiman's "Proving Ground", Fritz 1996, or Stern. Everything below is from pages/PDFs actually retrieved. Wikipedia and ETHW are pointers only and have thin or circular sourcing. Where a WebSearch summary asserted something I could not confirm in a fetched page, I omitted it.

## What do Wescoff's own interviews say about education, joining, first job, and supervisor?

### Takeaway
I found no transcript of Wescoff's own words. The only near-first-person route is Fritz's 1996 interviews, seen only through a 2011 AAUW-Illinois newsletter that cites Fritz and eniacprogrammers.org. It says she graduated in 1942 from Temple's Secondary Education Department with a social studies degree, and that a friend told her the Moore School was hiring.

### Cited Findings
- AAUW-Illinois newsletter (May 2011) says she graduated from Temple's Secondary Education Department in 1942 with a social studies degree, where jobs were scarce; a friend told her the Moore School was hiring — [AAUW-IL "CountHerhistory" May 2011 PDF](https://aauw-il.aauw.net/files/2013/04/May11.pdf) (its stated sources: eniacprogrammers.org and W. Barkley Fritz, "The Women of ENIAC"). Quality: secondary/tertiary newsletter, but traceable to Fritz's interview-based article.
- Same source: knowing how to run a calculator and operate an adding machine got her a job "to work on program computing"; she was shown a Marchant calculator at once and took calculus and trigonometry classes — [AAUW-IL PDF](https://aauw-il.aauw.net/files/2013/04/May11.pdf). The newsletter spells her "Mary Wescoff Meltzer", a clear error, which lowers reliability.
- ETHW and I-Programmer (and Wikipedia, citing the ETHW/IEEE GHN wiki page) say: Temple graduate 1942; hired by Moore School for weather calculations because she could operate an adding machine; moved to ballistic trajectory calculations in 1943 — [ETHW Marlyn Meltzer](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer); [I Programmer, Sue Gee](https://www.i-programmer.info/history/9-machines/17963-eniacs-women-programmers-.html?start=3); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer). These are one lineage (ETHW has no references), so they count as a single weak source, not three.
- Eniacprogrammers.org lists Wescoff Meltzer among four women interviewed on camera (Kleiman and David Roland oral histories) but the page gives no biographical content — [documentary info](https://eniacprogrammers.org/documentary-info/). Her biography page at eniacprogrammers.org/marlyn-wescoff-meltzer/ had no education/recruitment text.
- No source I retrieved names her supervisor. Mauchly names John Holberton as supervisor of the desk-calculator integration group (see below).

### Inferences
- The "social studies / secondary education" degree (AAUW) is compatible with Mauchly's description of someone trained to teach high-school history. The ETHW/Wikipedia "mathematics major, minor in business machines" claim appears in a search-engine summary only (not found in the pages fetched), so it is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Full text of Fritz 1996, Wescoff's 1997 Kleiman interview, Computer History Museum or Penn oral histories (none found for her), Bartik, Kleiman, Stern: not accessed.
- Whether she says anything about Mauchly, a Signal Corps project, Adele Goldstine or Gillon: unknown.

## What does Mauchly's 1973 oral history say (the document in question)?

### Takeaway
Mauchly's passage is verified in the transcript. He says he hired "Marlene Wescott" as one of "my computers" on a Signal Corps project, and does not say where she was trained as a teacher beyond "history".

### Cited Findings
- Transcript wording: "Another one, Marlene Wescott, who married a dentist by the name of Phil... had trained to be a teacher of high school history. Her major was history; I don't remember what school." — [Mauchly interview, 10 Jan 1973, Smithsonian NMAH AC0196](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf) (Tropp interviewer; I extracted the PDF text myself; quote is the "interesting person of an entirely different sort" passage). Quality: primary, but a recollection 30 years later with admitted gaps.
- He says: "I hired her... as one of my computers on this Signal Corps project to do these step by step integrations," not knowing "how she got to come to us or who recommended her." Same source.
- He says she needed no mathematics for the tabular desk-calculator work, was error-free, was later "transferred to the numerical integration work" (differential analyzer wording is his), and "volunteered and was accepted by Goldstine apparently" for ENIAC. Same source.
- Context he gives: Kay McNulty and Frances Bilas were the first Civil Service hires for hand Friden-calculator trajectories, later volunteering to the differential analyzer; Betty Snyder came as a desk-calculator employee under supervisor John Holberton; Ruth Lichterman "came into the group some way" and he did not know her until chosen. Same source. Note Mauchly's list covers exactly the six names of the programmers, with Jennings apparently not discussed in this passage.
- The "Phil" dentist: Mauchly gives no surname. No other source I retrieved gives Wescoff's marriage details beyond Meltzer as married name (she resigned in 1947 to marry per ETHW/Wikipedia). Whether her husband was named Phil/Philip: not verified here.

### Inferences
- Match evidence is partial: (a) the transcript's "Marlene Wescott" is in a list of the six ENIAC programmers and sits between Snyder and Lichterman, which is only possible if she is the sixth woman (Wescoff); (b) the six names are otherwise fully accounted for (McNulty, Bilas, Snyder, Lichterman; Jennings was a later hire). This is inference from list structure, not a source statement. (c) Teaching-track/social-studies degree matches "teacher of high school history".
- Mismatch/uncertainty: weather calculations (1942) in ETHW/Wikipedia vs Mauchly's "Signal Corps project step-by-step integrations". Not resolved.

### Gaps
- No second source confirms Wescoff worked on Mauchly's Signal Corps antenna project. No source uses "Marlene" or "Wescott" other than Mauchly's transcript (my searches found none).

## How was the 1942-43 cohort recruited, and is the Signal Corps group distinguished from BRL's?

### Takeaway
Secondary literature describes a BRL desk-calculator group and a differential-analyzer group at the Moore School, with Goldstine picking six from both; I found no account that separates a Signal Corps antenna group.

### Cited Findings
- Light (1999) citing the Goldstine interview: at BRL "one group of women used desk calculators and another the differential analyzer"; Goldstine chose six, who reported to Holberton; six named include Marlyn Wescoff (reporting July 1945) — [Light, "When Computers Were Women", Technology & Culture 40(3)](https://cs.brown.edu/courses/cs1951i/lightWhenComputersWereWomen.pdf) (text extracted from PDF; I searched for antenna/Signal Corps and Wescoff/Meltzer: only the Wescoff mentions at the ENIAC-selection passage and a 1946 press caption). Quality: high (peer-reviewed).
- Search summary of Light/ETHW: Army 1942 notice sought women math majors; Adele Goldstine and Mary Mauchly helped recruit/train — [ETHW Women Computers in World War II](https://ethw.org/Women_Computers_in_World_War_II) (listed in search results; I did not fetch it, so treat as unverified).
- Fritz via AAUW: Kay McNulty told a fellow student the Army was recruiting math majors — [AAUW-IL PDF](https://aauw-il.aauw.net/files/2013/04/May11.pdf).
- Press caption (1946) gives "Miss Marlyn Wescoff (Philadelphia, Pa.)" — quoted in Light's article (same URL as above), confirms name spelling.

### Inferences
- Mauchly's Signal Corps step-by-step integration work and the BRL firing-table work were both desk-calculator, tabular integrations in the same Moore School pool, so a hire under his project may still have been counted in the "computers" group Light describes.

### Gaps
- Not fetched: Bartik "Pioneer Programmer", Kleiman "Proving Ground", Fritz 1996 itself (the UW course PDF link returned a login page), McCartney, Stern, Goldstine's "The Computer from Pascal to von Neumann" (cited by Light, n. 41), Kay McNulty and Holberton oral histories (e.g., CHM, Smithsonian), Penn Today. Wescoff, "Marlene" or "Wescott" elsewhere: none found.
