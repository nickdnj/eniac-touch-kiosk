# Marlyn Wescoff (Meltzer) biography vs. Mauchly's "Marlene Wescott"

Method note: web search and page fetches only, about 15 calls. I could NOT read Kleiman's *Proving Ground*, Bartik's memoir, Light, the Fritz article (scanned PDF, unreadable by fetch), or any primary interview transcript. Everything below is secondary or tertiary. Source ratings: P = primary, S = secondary, T = tertiary/aggregator.

## 1. Education: college, major, graduation year

### Takeaway
Temple University, 1942 is consistent across all sources. The only authoritative-institution source that names a field says "social studies" (Penn Today). One tertiary page says mathematics with business-machines minor. No source I found says history major or teacher training.

### Cited Findings
- Graduated Temple in 1942, "with a degree in social studies" (Penn Today, University of Pennsylvania's own news office, 2021, ENIAC 75th anniversary piece). Rating S, institutional. — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/worlds-first-general-purpose-computer-turns-75)
- Graduated Temple 1942, major not stated — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer) (T; cites Kleiman 2022 p. 252, ETHW, IEEE, WITI, da Cruz); [ETHW](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer) (T/S); [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer) (T); [i-programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/history/9-machines/17963-eniacs-women-programmers-.html?start=3) (T)
- "Mathematics major, business machines minor" (or "commercial machines" specialization) — [EcuRed](https://www.ecured.cu/Marlyn_Wescoff_Meltzer) (T, Spanish wiki; its only listed source is ETHW, which itself does NOT say this, so the claim is unsupported/likely embellished). A search summary attributed the same claim to an unnamed "profile"; I did not locate the original.
- No fetched source mentions history, teaching, or a teaching certificate. Find a Grave page explicitly lacks "teacher" and "dentist".

### Inferences
- "Social studies" at Temple in 1942 is compatible with a history/social-science track (and Temple's education college), so Penn Today does not contradict Mauchly's "trained as a high school history teacher" but does not confirm it either. The math-major claim is weak (single wiki chain).
- Penn Today's source for "social studies" is unstated; possibly Kleiman's work.

### Gaps
- Temple registrar/yearbook (Templar 1942) not checked; Kleiman's book and the Wescoff interviews not read; whether degree was from Teachers College or College of Liberal Arts unknown.

## 2. Birth, family, later life, death

### Takeaway
Born 2 Mar 1922 Philadelphia; died December 2008 in Yardley, Bucks County, PA; date conflicts (4 vs 7 Dec).

### Cited Findings
- Born 2 Mar 1922, Philadelphia — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer)
- Died Yardley, PA, age 86; header says 4 Dec 2008, obituary text says "Thursday, December 7, 2008" — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer); Wikipedia says 7 Dec. (Note 7 Dec 2008 was a Sunday, so the "Thursday" in the obituary fits neither date; 4 Dec 2008 was a Thursday, supporting 4 Dec. My inference from the calendar, not from a source.)
- Survivors: son Hugh Meltzer, daughter Joy Levine (husband Craig Levine), grandchildren Aaron Webb-Meltzer, Aaron and Sarah Levine, godson Scott Caster — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer) (obituary excerpt; the Legacy.com original returned 403)
- Later life: volunteered at Shir Ami library and Sunday-school story hour, Meals on Wheels 10+ years, Hadassah treasurer, knitted 500+ chemo hats; inducted WITI Hall of Fame 1997 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer); [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer)
- Parents/siblings: not found in any fetched source.

### Gaps
- Parents, siblings, Inquirer obituary text, SSDI not found. WikiTree page (https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Wescoff-1) returned empty on fetch; search snippet only.

## 3. Husband and marriage

### Takeaway
Husband was Dr. Philip Meltzer (1918-1997), married 2 Feb 1947 in Philadelphia. His profession is NOT stated in anything I could read. "Phil" matches Mauchly's "Phil", but the dentist claim is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Married Philip Meltzer 2 Feb 1947, Philadelphia, officiated by Rabbi Issachar Levin; one son, one daughter — WikiTree (T; genealogy, via search snippet only, page itself not retrievable): https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Wescoff-1
- "Dr. Philip Meltzer," 1918-1997, listed as husband; profession not stated — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992/marlyn-meltzer)
- Left the ENIAC team in 1947 to marry — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer); [ETHW](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer); [EcuRed](https://www.ecured.cu/Marlyn_Wescoff_Meltzer) (names "Dr. Philip Meltzer", T, derived)
- "Louis Meltzer": no source found; appears to be a guess in the question.

### Inferences
- Resignation to marry (1947) fits Mauchly's "married a dentist by the name of Phil". Mauchly's interview (1973) postdates, so he could know. The "Dr." plus first name Phil is a notable match; a doctorate title is consistent with DDS but equally with MD.

### Gaps
- Husband's profession: need Inquirer obituary for Philip Meltzer 1997, PA dental/medical licensure, 1950 census (Philadelphia), or Kleiman's book. Searches found nothing.

## 4. Hiring and job titles over time

### Takeaway
Hired by the Moore School in 1942 for weather (meteorological) calculations because she could run an adding machine; moved to ballistic trajectory work in 1943; picked for ENIAC programming 1945. No source I read mentions Signal Corps antenna/radar integrations.

### Cited Findings
- Hired Moore School after Temple, weather calculations, mainly because she could operate an adding machine; 1943 ballistics trajectories on desk calculators; 1945 one of six ENIAC programmers — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer); [ETHW](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer) (dates hiring to 1942); [i-programmer](https://www.i-programmer.info/history/9-machines/17963-eniacs-women-programmers-.html?start=3)
- Her weather unit was disbanded, and she was encouraged to apply for civil service to stay on ballistics work — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/worlds-first-general-purpose-computer-turns-75)
- EcuRed: "about a year" after meteorological work was hired for ballistic trajectories; Army selected her for ENIAC 1945 — [EcuRed](https://www.ecured.cu/Marlyn_Wescoff_Meltzer)
- Titles: "computer" (human), then ENIAC programmer; Wikipedia infobox lists employer Moore School/Penn.

### Inferences
- Moore School weather calculations in 1942 were a separate project from BRL firing tables; Mauchly's own 1942-43 role (he taught and supervised) could plausibly include supervising a "step by step integration" computing group, but no fetched source ties Wescoff to Mauchly or to Signal Corps.

### Gaps
- Who hired her, and whether the "weather" work was a Signal Corps contract under Mauchly: not in any accessible source. Kleiman's *Proving Ground* (2022) and the ENIAC Programmers Project interviews (1997 Wescoff interview) are the likeliest places and were not accessible.

## 5. Unattributed claim to discard

- A search summary claimed "a master's degree with a major in English and a minor in education, returned to full-time work in 1967 as an editor" for Wescoff. No fetched page supports this for Wescoff (Wikipedia, ETHW, EcuRed, Find a Grave all silent); a follow-up search associated those details with Jean Bartik (Penn English master's, 1967, Auerbach). Treat as contamination from Bartik, not evidence about Wescoff.

## Bottom line for the identity question
Matches: Temple 1942 (a college graduate, plausible teacher-track), degree in "social studies" (adjacent to history), married in 1947 to a "Dr. Philip (Phil) Meltzer." Name "Marlene Wescott" vs "Marlyn Wescoff" is a plausible mishearing/transcription (one source reports "Westcott" as a variant). Unverified: husband a dentist; history major; Mauchly hiring. No source contradicts the match. Wikidata (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q49245) and the second-tier sources were not individually inspected.
