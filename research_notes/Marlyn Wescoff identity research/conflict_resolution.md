### Takeaway
Find a Grave memorial 280641824 (Philip Meltzer, 16 Oct 1918 - 10 Nov 1997) transcribes an obituary that calls him a dentist, Army captain, Temple dental school 1948 graduate, husband of Marlyn Wescoff Meltzer. So the note saying no source states his profession is wrong. Caveat: the fetch tool returned paraphrase, not verbatim text, so exact quotes were not captured.

No wedding date on the page. Died 10 Nov 1997 at St. Mary Medical Center, Middletown PA. Burial Fountain Lawn Memorial Park, Ewing NJ. — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/280641824)
- The obituary says age 69, which conflicts with the 1918 birth date (age 79); likely an error in the original notice. — same page
- Marlyn's memorial 211908992 links "Dr. Philip Meltzer (1918-1997)" as spouse; children Hugh and Joy (married to Craig Levine). It does not itself say dentist. Memorial header death date 4 Dec 2008 vs obituary text "Thursday, December 7, 2008" (internal conflict). Born 2 Mar 1922 Philadelphia. — [Find a Grave](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/211908992)

### Inferences
- Mauchly's 1973 "dentist named Phil" matches Philip Meltzer, DDS, so it supports the identification of Mauchly's "Marlene Wescott" with Marlyn Wescoff Meltzer. Strong but not independent proof, since both are circumstantial on first name and profession.

## 2. The list of the six (Mauchly/Tropp interview, 10 Jan 1973)

### Takeaway
Mauchly names "Marlene Wescott", who "married a dentist by the name of Phil" and was hired by him for the Signal Corps project, and says she later volunteered for ENIAC. He never says "Wescoff"; his list otherwise matches the six, so Wescott is the sixth-woman slot occupied by Wescoff. Source: [Smithsonian transcript PDF](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf), pdftotext of 73 pages (primary source; spelling and memory errors are Mauchly's/transcriber's).

### Cited Findings (names in order, PDF page)
- p.68: Frances Bilas; "Kathleen McNulty, who's now my wife"; Bilas "now Mrs. Homer Spence"; both math majors from Chestnut Hill, Civil Service, hand Friden calculators, then differential analyzer; he notes some other analyzer girls never transferred to ENIAC.
- p.69: Betty Snyder (later Holberton; he mentions husband John Holberton supervised the six); then "Marlene Wescott" (see below).
- p.70: Ruth Lichterman (married name Teitelbaum); "There were six of them there. Who have we lost?"; Tropp raises Adele Goldstine (not one of the six); continues p.71 with Adele Goldstine, Mildred Kramer, then "a sixth girl on the ENIAC programming project, Betty Jennings", later Betty Bartik.
- Wescott on p.69: "Marlene Wescott, who married a dentist by the name of Phil"; "can't remember his last name"; trained as high school history teacher, history major, school not remembered. "I hired her" as one of his computers "on this Signal Corps project" doing step by step integrations; he doesn't know how she came or who recommended her.
- p.70 on skill: "She never made a mistake!"; "like an automaton"; "number perfect"; no great mathematics understanding.
- p.70 on move: "after she was transferred to the numerical integration work and did well at it, she volunteered and was accepted by Goldstine apparently, to go to the ENIAC". He says she "had no interest in mathematics".

Does Wescott appear among those he identifies as ENIAC programmers? Yes: she is explicitly among the six (McNulty, Bilas, Snyder, Wescott, Lichterman, Jennings = six; "Wescoff" spelling never appears). Moving from Signal Corps work: he says she was "transferred to the numerical integration work", then went to ENIAC via Goldstine; he does not name the Ballistics Research Laboratory for her.

### Inferences
- Six names total; Wescott fills the slot of Marlyn Wescoff. Together with the dentist husband (Q1) the identification is very likely correct. Mauchly's "Marlene" is a recall error; Wescott a variant spelling.
- Mauchly's claim he hired her conflicts with some accounts that Goldstine/BRL selected her; not checked here.

### Gaps
- No page states "BRL" for her specifically.
