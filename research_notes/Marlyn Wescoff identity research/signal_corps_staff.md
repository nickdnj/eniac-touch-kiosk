# Signal Corps antenna contract staff at the Moore School (1941-1943) and the Wescott/Wescoff question

Method note: Mauchly transcript was downloaded and text-extracted locally (pdftotext); quotes below are from that text. ~10 tool calls; several planned sources (other AC0196 transcripts, Moore School annual reports, Stern, Goldstine, McCartney, Fritz, Penn archives) were NOT reached. See Gaps.

## Which people were hired as computers/staff on the Signal Corps antenna project?

### Takeaway
The only primary source found that names project personnel is Mauchly's 10 Jan 1973 Tropp interview. It names Brainerd, Burks, Sharpless, Witmer (and an unnamed emeritus physics professor) for the project, and "Marlene Wescott" as one of Mauchly's computers on it. No complete staff list with dates was found.

### Cited Findings
- Brainerd negotiated a contract with "the Signal Corps in New Jersey" to study a parabolic reflector with a dipole radiator at its focus (radar pattern sharpness); Mauchly says he never saw the contract and cannot recall the time limit (Brainerd promised roughly 6 months). Numerical integration of electromagnetic equations was the method. — [Mauchly/Tropp, 10 Jan 1973, NMAH AC0196](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf) (primary, oral history recalled 30 years later; high value but memory-based)
- The contract was "in part experimental"; Arthur Burks was assigned to it, worked partly with the physical/experimental people and kept in touch with Mauchly's numerical work. Sharpless "worked on this Signal Corps project, on the experimental end." — same source
- Brainerd gave Mauchly Stratton's electromagnetic text and an emeritus physics professor as a helper (unnamed; Mauchly says he was no help); then Professor Witmer of Physics was put in touch, but Mauchly says Witmer was not much help. Mauchly "believe[s]" Witmer was brought in for the radiation-pattern job. — same source
- Mauchly laid out tabular forms; to do the many small-element calculations he needed more help. Part-time students were available for hire, and the project was short of desk calculators (Moore School had one; he tried to borrow Ursinus's Monroe). — same source
- Mauchly says he had not finished the Signal Corps radiation-pattern computations when the ENIAC project started, so he completed some during early ENIAC work (i.e., 1943). — same source
- Among Mauchly's "computers" on the project: "Marlene Wescott," who married a dentist named Phil, trained as a high-school history teacher, history major; "I hired her ... as one of my computers on this Signal Corps project." Mauchly said she needed no mathematics for the tabular desk-calculator integrations, was checked by others and "never made a mistake," and was later transferred to the numerical integration (BRL trajectory) work, then volunteered and was accepted by Goldstine for ENIAC. — same source (end of transcript, final pages)
- Neither Mauchly nor Tropp's text (as extracted) names Camp Evans; "Signal Corps in New Jersey" is the only location given. — same source
- Related secondary summary: the Signal Corps work was designated project PZ, with Brainerd, Burks and Mauchly involved. — [InfoAge, Camp Evans/ENIAC page](https://infoage.org/eniac-development-impelled-by-camp-evans-project/) (secondary, museum; not verified against primary; PZ label seen only via search snippet)

### Inferences
- The project staff Mauchly names is small: Brainerd (PI/negotiator), Mauchly (theory/numerical), Burks and Sharpless (experimental), Witmer and an unnamed emeritus professor (advisors), plus desk-calculator computers, of whom only "Wescott" is named.
- The other early desk-calculator "girls" Mauchly names (McNulty, Bilas, Snyder, Lichterman) are described as BRL trajectory computers, not Signal Corps; he does not say they worked on the antenna job.

### Gaps
- No full roster, hiring dates, or contract number/dates found. Not checked: Eckert, Burks, Brainerd, Sharpless, Goldstine oral histories; Mauchly 6 Feb 1973 session; Moore School annual reports; Penn archives; Stern; Goldstine; McCartney; Burks & Burks; Fritz.

## Does any source say Marlyn Wescoff worked on the Signal Corps/antenna calculations before ENIAC, or only BRL firing tables?

### Takeaway
Only Mauchly says Signal Corps. Biographical sources say weather calculations then ballistics trajectories, with conflicting dates. The "Wescott" in Mauchly's account is very probably Marlyn Wescoff, because he places her among the six ENIAC programmers.

### Cited Findings
- In Mauchly's walk through the six programmers, he lists McNulty, Bilas, Snyder, then "Marlene Wescott," then Lichterman, then Betty Jennings ("a sixth girl"). Her later move to ENIAC via Goldstine matches the programmer story. Note the first name differs (Marlene vs Marlyn) and surname spelled Wescott. — [Mauchly/Tropp 1973](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC0196_mauc730110.pdf)
- Mauchly's description (married a dentist named Phil; history/social-studies type background) is consistent with Marlyn Wescoff Meltzer (married Phil Meltzer per later biographies; Temple graduate, social studies per some sources). The "dentist" detail was NOT independently verified in what I fetched. — [ETHW Marlyn Meltzer](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlyn_Meltzer) (secondary, tertiary)
- ETHW: hired by the Moore School in 1942 after Temple, for weather calculations because she knew how to operate an adding machine; in 1943 reassigned to ballistics trajectories, working among ~80 women "computers." — [ETHW Marlyn Meltzer](https://ethw.org/Marlyn_Meltzer) (secondary; source citation not given on the page)
- Other biographical summaries repeat 1942 Temple graduation, weather work, then ballistics 1943; they differ on degree (social studies vs mathematics) and on year chosen as programmer (1943 vs 1945). — [Jewish Women's Archive via search](https://cdn.jwa.org/encyclopedia/article/jewish-women-in-computer-science); [ITHistory honor roll](https://www.ithistory.org/honor-roll/mrs-marlyn-meltzer-nee-wescoff) (secondary; only seen via search snippets, not fetched)
- No source found says she was hired for BRL firing tables only; none other than Mauchly says Signal Corps. "Weather calculations" in 1942 is a further unexplained discrepancy (possible garbling of the Signal Corps/antenna work, speculation only).

### Inferences
- Reconciling: 1942 hire for non-ballistics computing (Mauchly: Signal Corps integrations; biographies: weather) then 1943 transfer to BRL trajectories (both sources agree on a move to trajectory integrations). Plausible but not documented by any source I found.
- Mauchly's "Wescott" and Wescoff are probably the same person; a distinct Wescott is unsupported.

### Gaps
- No contemporaneous (1942-43) personnel record found. Meltzer's own oral histories (e.g., Kleiman/ENIAC Programmers Project, Fritz) not reached. Her own account of her first assignment is the key missing item.

## Any other person named Wescott/Wescoff (or similar) at the Moore School 1941-1946; what do other AC0196 transcripts say?

### Takeaway
None found.

### Cited Findings
- Text search of the AC0196 abstracts PDFs for e-g, i-m and s-z returned no "Wescoff"/"Wescott" hits. — [e-g](https://amhistory.si.edu/archives/AC0196_abstracts_e-g.pdf), [i-m](https://amhistory.si.edu/archives/AC0196_abstracts_i-m.pdf), [s-z](https://amhistory.si.edu/archives/AC0196_abstracts_s-z.pdf) (finding-aid abstracts, partial; a-d and n-r not checked)
- Within the Mauchly 10 Jan 1973 transcript, the only occurrence of "Wesco*" is the "Marlene Wescott" passage. — same transcript
- ETHW "Women Computers in World War II" lists Marlyn Wescoff and does not mention Signal Corps antenna work or any Wescott. — [ETHW](https://ethw.org/Women_Computers_in_World_War_II)

### Inferences
- Abstract absence is weak evidence: abstracts are summaries, not full text.

### Gaps
